London, in thirty-three parts

London was never planned as one city. It grew out of a Roman port, a royal abbey and hundreds of Saxon farms, fords and clearings, and in 1965 an Act of Parliament joined them up. This is a trip through all thirty-three pieces: who named them, who built them, and the odd stories they still keep.

Place names: the meanings follow the standard reference works on English place names, above all A. D. Mills’s Dictionary of London Place-Names. Old English personal names are reconstructions, and scholars still argue about several of them.

History: where a story is tradition rather than record, the text says “said to”, “tradition says” or similar. London likes its legends and most of them are worth repeating, so long as you say what they are.
